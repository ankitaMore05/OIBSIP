Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

The scientist who taught a nation to dream.

A life of learning

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. His family had little money, and as a boy he sold newspapers to help at home. His curiosity about birds and flight turned into a lifelong love of science.

He studied physics and then aerospace engineering, and began his career in defence research. He later joined ISRO, where he led the team behind India's first indigenous satellite launch vehicle, the SLV-III. This work earned him the nickname "Missile Man of India" for his role in the country's missile programmes.

In 2002 he became the 11th President of India, and people affectionately called him the "People's President". He kept teaching throughout his term and afterwards, because he believed young minds shaped the future of the nation.

He wrote several books, including Wings of Fire, which has inspired many students. He passed away on 27 July 2015 while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong, doing what he loved most.

Key milestones

  1. 1931 Born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.
  2. 1980 SLV-III launches the Rohini satellite into orbit.
  3. 1997 Awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.
